# Wiki and Church Documents

WIKI-001–011, DOC-001–023 and BRD-001–006 define current reading, personal reading state, source editing, safe exports, template generation, template themes and the church brand kit, and native owned writing. Wiki organization editorial rights and Church Documents rights are separate. Plant values/guides/internal writing uses Church Documents; there is no plant Wiki editor or third CMS. Native and Evry use the same current-entitlement resolver; each durable Evry write is exactly reviewed.

## Article resolution and editing

Each article has a canonical logical identity independent of title or slug. Each source has global or exact organization ownership, with immutable published versions and safe content hashes. Independent same-title/slug additions receive distinct logical IDs. Organization editing of a global article keeps its canonical ID and creates only that organization's version. The resolver returns selected published version, hidden-default, source_order_required or unavailable.

Resolve each article independently: first eligible explicit published edit/hide in the plant's stored sending-church order, then sending-network order, then global. Inherited/unchanged/missing/inactive sources fall through. Each group has one explicit sequence; its first entry is Preferred Wiki source. Setting preference moves the chosen eligible entry to head while preserving the rest, through the same revisioned `wiki.setSourceOrder` operation. Preference grants no affiliation primacy, oversight, financing or editorial authority. Invalid duplicate/foreign/wrong-kind entries and changed reviewed state fail closed.

A winning hide suppresses the default article in list/search/navigation/related/guide/export/retrieval. An authorized source-view browser and Source dropdown still expose other independently eligible published versions, including global. The hiding source's hidden body and drafts are denied. Switching source is a read context; it does not mutate plant defaults. Unique eligible additions remain discoverable. Missing explicit order for competing sources displays source_order_required, never a name/ID/date/join-order winner. An added/rejoining affiliation recognition is a new unranked candidate; administrative explicit order resolves competition, while authorized source reading/unique additions remain. Existing inactive entries are skipped without automatically rewriting history or restoring old ranks.

Current own-organization Owner/Admin may add/edit/hide/publish its versions. No plant administrator edits global or another organization; global publication is scoped reference-operator authority. Native publishing uses expected state/version, sanitized shared editor content and meaningful-update flag; mutation/history, audit and the recorded outcome and required upstream notice agree transactionally. Global publication flows through unedited inheritance, preserving organization customizations/hides. It issues an in-app-only upstream-review notice to current organization editors for every changed published global revision, including typo-only. That notice never adopts/overwrites or becomes email due to preferences. Publisher-marked meaningful revision controls the separate reader label, not the editor trigger.

All body access is current: direct route/ID, search/snippet/count, navigation, related, source view, guide, PDF, old conflict detection/history/citation, cache, stream, generated context and Evry retrieval. Ending one affiliation removes its bodies/history while surviving independently valid grants remain. Old bookmarks/completion/citations retain minimal identity evidence but cannot restore content. Hosted prior references redact denied excerpts and offer a current eligible source, rather than replay departed text into UI/model. Already downloaded/public external copies cannot be recalled. Cache keys bind purpose, exact scope, article/source/version and current affiliation/order/publication/access revision; private/public caches cannot share keys. Reauthorize on retrieval and response/stream continuation; invalidation alone is not a permission check.

## Reading and Discovery evidence

Completion retains actual reader, canonical article, source/version/hash, policy version, completion time and observed progress. A publisher-marked meaningful revision after that evidence shows **Updated since you read it** while keeping completed credit and allowing voluntary reread. Typo-only changes do not create the reader label; an intervening typo cannot erase an unread earlier meaningful update. Existing completion never claims the reader saw new text, resets Discovery/training or bypasses current body entitlement.

Discovery foundations is Met only when **one eligible actual reader has completed all six canonical Discovery articles at a ratio strictly greater than 0.80 for each**. An actual Owner, or an ordinary lead linked to the actual reader account with a current same-plant Admin seat, can supply the whole set under the accepted current authority. Ordinary Admin title alone cannot qualify. CRM lead title creates no authority. Owner 0/6 plus eligible actual lead/reader 6/6 qualifies through that one reader; two people each reading disjoint halves does not. Credits are never pooled. Any currently accessible published source version of the same canonical article qualifies; drafts, hidden/departed body access and unrelated same-title articles do not.

This is the same progress proxy, with no exercise/quiz/understanding gate. The six-article mapping is explicit rather than phase=0's larger corpus.

Before creation the account reads the entire currently published global Wiki, not only the six foundation articles, and has native private documents but no phantom church/plant phase. ## Template themes, classes and copy

The format rule is: budgets are XLSX, emails are HTML mail, fixed-layout documents are PDF only, and editable prose documents are edited in the app and exported as PDF and DOCX. The Forme engine (@formepdf) renders PDFs with fillable fields and PDF/UA tagging. The named catalog lives in [`document-templates.json`](../catalog/document-templates.json). It holds ids, classes, formats, phases and priority only, with no template content.

| Feature | Rule |
|---|---|
| DOC-017 | Themed document rendering with the neutral fallback. Generated PDF and email templates render through a document theme. `resolveDocumentTheme(church, documentClass)` returns the church theme when the church has a brand kit and the document's "use church branding" toggle is on. Otherwise it returns the Everyfield-neutral theme. WP-036 renders the neutral theme and accepts a theme input; the church theme needs the WP-104 brand kit. A theme change applies only to newly generated documents, and saved files keep their exact bytes. |
| DOC-018 | Document classes. Every template has exactly one document class: formal/legal, outward-facing, operational or email. The class decides how the theme applies. `docs/catalog/document-templates.json` records the class of each template. Policies, bylaws and agreements are formal/legal; the prospectus and letters are outward-facing; budgets, checklists and agendas are operational; every email is class email. |
| DOC-019 | Legal template notice. Every formal/legal template shows the notice "Template, not legal advice" in the preview and in the saved PDF. Church branding cannot hide or remove the notice. Templates of other classes do not show it. A Church Document made from a formal/legal template keeps the notice too. The notice "Template, not legal advice" renders at export, outside the editable body, in both the PDF and the DOCX. No edit removes it. Exports of other classes do not show it. |
| DOC-020 | Doctrinal sections as network-filled slots. A doctrinal section in a template is an empty slot. Everyfield ships no doctrinal text. The sending network fills the slot for its churches, and a church without a network fills it itself. A slot that no one has filled generates as a visible blank, never as default text. In a Church Document, a doctrinal slot is a node that editing can fill but cannot delete. The sending network's current text fills the slot at creation when the network has filled it. An empty slot shows as a marked blank in the editor and exports as a visible blank in the PDF and the DOCX, never as default text. |
| DOC-021 | Template copy rule. Template text says "church plant" and never a bare "plant". Template text spells the brand "Everyfield". The rule covers every template, notice and email. |

A template renders with the Everyfield-neutral theme when the church has no brand kit, so WP-036 ships without WP-104. The church theme comes from the [church brand kit](#church-brand-kit).

## Church brand kit

A church sets its identity once. Generated documents and the email class then use it, within the limits of each document class. The Church group of Settings holds the page. A church with no kit keeps the neutral theme, so no document waits on the kit.

| Feature | Complete current behavior |
|---|---|
| BRD-001 | Brand settings. Each church has one brand kit. Edit it at `/settings/brand`, in the Church group of the Settings dialog. Only the Owner and Admin can open or change it. Controls sit on the left and a live preview sits on the right, with one preview tab per document class (formal/legal, outward-facing, operational and email). Reset to default clears the kit and returns the church to the Everyfield-neutral theme. |
| BRD-002 | Logo assets. The kit holds a full logo (about 3:1) and a square mark, each with an optional light-on-dark variant. Upload accepts SVG, PNG and WebP, trims empty margin and rasterizes the file. Files use private storage with the `brand_asset` purpose, reusing the avatar upload pattern. Every logo needs alt text. The preview shows each logo on light, dark and brand-color backgrounds. |
| BRD-003 | Colors and contrast. The kit has a required primary color and an accent color that falls back to the primary color. The page suggests colors from the uploaded logo. A contrast badge shows the WCAG contrast ratio and the text color derived for each brand color. A brand color under 3:1 against its background is never used as text. |
| BRD-004 | Fonts. Heading and body fonts come from a curated list of open-license fonts. The defaults are DM Serif for headings and DM Sans for body text. Version 1 has no font upload. |
| BRD-005 | Letterhead details. The kit holds legal name, address, website, phone, email, EIN and tagline. It also holds an optional cover photo and an optional signature block. |

## Native owned writing

DOC-016 `document` has exactly one immutable owner: account or plant; `document_revision` inherits that owner through scoped FK. Creator attribution is not ownership. Account-private original/read/import objects stay accessible only to that active account. Plant-scoped Church Documents use current document read/write predicates under accepted plant authority; organization Wiki grants/affiliation/sharing alone never disclose them. No broad team/org grant is created by reuse of editor components.

Reuse the Wiki safe editor for native text/headings/lists/tables/links, save/render/edit, explicit `core_values` designation and revision/archive history. Ready saved nonempty designated current native text is structural core-values evidence; empty/title-only/unsaved/archived/failed parse never qualifies. No model judges theology/quality/calling/compliance or approves publication. Corrections append attributed revisions and expected-current-revision current-state check. Archive removes current evidence while retaining why prior assessments used a historical revision.

Import supports UTF-8 text and modern Word DOCX parsed into editable native content, not an attachment-only link. Reject legacy binary DOC, macro-enabled documents, unsupported or malformed containers with a clear format/retry path. Engineering bounds: upload ≤10 MiB, DOCX expanded ≤40 MiB, ≤2,000 ZIP entries, ≤2 million extracted text characters and 15-second parser timeout. Check container signatures/path traversal/expansion, discard scripts/macros/external relationships/embedded executables, never fetch remote resources, and sanitize native nodes/URLs. Preserve private import provenance/source hash. Failed/empty import leaves prior ready revision/evidence intact. No OCR/conversion service or perfect Word layout promise.

The explicit native **Use for this church** command copies one selected authorized account revision into one actual authorized plant-owned document with immutable source lineage, preserving the private original. It never copies all account assets, transfers ownership silently or guesses a plant in a multi-context account. The selected copy becomes plant-owned under normal document policy; later original edits do not silently update it. The copy, history, audit and reported outcome succeed or fail together and any byte references follow the upload lifecycle. Plant-owned docs survive creator-account deletion; account-owned originals follow that account's private deletion lifecycle. Private care is never a document/model source.

### Church Documents from editable templates

Text-heavy templates are prose, so a church edits them in Everyfield. An editable template makes a Church Document, and the Church Document exports as a themed PDF or a Word file. The catalog marks each template `editable` in [`document-templates.json`](../catalog/document-templates.json). Fixed-layout documents stay field-driven PDFs, budgets stay XLSX and emails stay HTML mail.

| Feature | Complete current behavior |
|---|---|
| DOC-022 | An editable template creates one Church Document owned by the church plant, under the DOC-016 owner, revision and archive rules. Revision 1 holds the template text with merge values that resolve once under the DOC-002 limits, so later contact or church data changes do not change the text. The document records the template id, template version, document class and frozen input provenance. It also holds a "use church branding" toggle. Only a current Owner or Admin of the church plant creates it. Creation makes no file. Fixed-layout, budget and email templates are refused. Before creation, `documents.preview` shows the pre-filled content as a PDF. |
| DOC-023 | A user who can read a template-made Church Document exports one revision as a PDF or a DOCX. The PDF comes from the Forme engine with PDF/UA tagging. Both formats use the theme that `resolveDocumentTheme(church, documentClass)` returns, the "use church branding" toggle and the Everyfield-neutral fallback. The DOCX carries the theme's logo with alt text, fonts, colors and letterhead footer where Word can show them. Each export saves one `generated_document` under DOC-003. That record holds the document id and revision as frozen provenance. A later download returns the exact saved bytes after later edits, brand kit changes or template changes. The DOC-002 limits apply to the export (8 MiB and 1,000 content blocks), and a render failure is truthful. Only a Church Document made from a template exports, because only these documents carry a document class. |

`documents.generate` makes files only for fixed-layout and budget templates and refuses an editable template. An editable template goes through `documents.createFromTemplate`, then `documents.exportNative`. The template preview stays for every template. Branding applies at export, not at creation, so a brand kit change never edits a saved document. Saved exports keep their exact bytes (DOC-002 and DOC-003). The legal notice (DOC-019) and doctrinal slots (DOC-020) hold under editing. Import of an edited DOCX back into the document is not part of this feature, and the DOC-016 import stays as written.
